Executive summary

  • 461,312 road accidents were reported in India in 2022 (+11.9% vs 2021).
  • 168,491 people were killed (+9.4% vs 2021).
  • Road deaths are dominated by Two-wheeler riders (44.5% of deaths).
  • ‘Over speeding’ was the recorded factor in 71.2% of deaths (recorded factor, not proven cause).
  • 79 passenger-car crash tests are catalogued; results are shown as protection points normalised within each protocol era — star ratings are deliberately not compared across protocols.

Scope & method

Primary question: what does the evidence say about passenger-car safety in India? National and state accident statistics provide the context; crash-test results provide the model-level, testable evidence. We keep three evidence types strictly separate: official statistics crash-test assessments derived indicators.

A police-recorded factor (e.g. ‘over speeding’) is a recorded factor, not proof of causation. Observational correlation is not treated as causation anywhere in this report.

Evidence grading

Each registered source is scored 0–100 on five independent evidence dimensions (primary source, corroboration, recency, completeness, identification confidence). The dimensions are deliberately kept separate — they are not collapsed into a single quality judgement; the mean is shown only as a convenience, never as a verdict on the publisher.

What this data does NOT support

  • Crash-test results describe a specific tested variant; other variants/trim levels may differ.
  • Star ratings are not compared across protocols, years or markets (different maxima and test routines).
  • National accident totals are police-recorded and subject to under-reporting and definitional differences.
  • A recorded contributing factor is not proof of causation.
  • Model-level real-world risk cannot be computed without per-model exposure (registrations/kilometres), which is not available here.

Methodology appendix

  • Collection is gated by a source registry: a source must be verified before it is fetched.
  • Every record carries provenance (source slug, retrieval time, method, content hash) and is stored append-only.
  • Missing values are stored as NULL and rendered as ‘n/a’ — never as zero.
  • Rates are computed from published numerators and denominators (e.g. deaths per lakh population from the MoRTH population column).
  • Crash-test protection is normalised only within the same stated protocol maximum.
This report is generated from public data with recorded provenance. Costs, counts and shares are reported as published; unavailable values are shown as ‘n/a’ and never replaced with zero. See the source register for licences and attribution.
